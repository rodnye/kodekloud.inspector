# Setting up your own AWS Account A walkthrough

Welcome, future solutions architects! In this lesson, we will guide you step-by-step through the process of registering for your very own AWS account. While KodeKloud provides AWS playgrounds for cost-free experimentation, setting up your own account enables you to explore additional services not available in the playgrounds.

> [!important]
> **Cost Management Reminder**
>
> AWS services may incur charges if left running (e.g., NAT gateways, virtual machines, volumes, network firewalls). Always remember to create and delete your resources as needed.

Let's dive into the registration process:

## Step 1: Sign Up for an AWS Account

Start by navigating to the [AWS homepage](https://aws.amazon.com/) and clicking on the "Sign up for a free AWS account" button.

![The image shows an AWS signup page where a user can enter their email to create a new account or sign in to an existing one. There is also a small video overlay of a person in the bottom right corner.](https://kodekloud.com/kk-media/image/upload/v1752864615/notes-assets/images/AWS-Solutions-Architect-Associate-Certification-Setting-up-your-own-AWS-Account-A-walkthrough/aws-signup-page-email-video-overlay.jpg)

When prompted, enter your email address. You can use a plus sign with an alias (e.g., if your email is Michael@KodeKloud.com, you could use Michael+AWS1@KodeKloud.com). Although it appears unique to AWS, emails will still be delivered to your main inbox. Next, choose an account name, such as "Michael's demo KK account," and verify your email.

## Step 2: Email Verification

AWS will send a verification code to your email. Once you receive that code, enter it and click "Verify."

![The image shows an AWS signup page prompting the user to enter a verification code sent to their email. There is also a small video call window in the bottom right corner.](https://kodekloud.com/kk-media/image/upload/v1752864616/notes-assets/images/AWS-Solutions-Architect-Associate-Certification-Setting-up-your-own-AWS-Account-A-walkthrough/aws-signup-verification-code.jpg)

After verification, you'll be asked to create a password for your root account. Choose a strong, memorable password that satisfies AWS's complexity requirements.

![The image shows a webpage for signing up for an AWS account, with instructions for creating a password. It includes password requirements and a confirmation that the email address has been verified.](https://kodekloud.com/kk-media/image/upload/v1752864617/notes-assets/images/AWS-Solutions-Architect-Associate-Certification-Setting-up-your-own-AWS-Account-A-walkthrough/aws-account-signup-password-requirements.jpg)

## Step 3: Entering Personal and Contact Information

Provide your personal details such as your full name, usage type (e.g., business or personal), and phone number. Additionally, fill in your billing address. For demonstration purposes, personal details may appear blurred for privacy.

![The image shows an AWS signup page with free tier offers and a form for contact information. A person is visible in a small video overlay at the bottom right corner.](https://kodekloud.com/kk-media/image/upload/v1752864618/notes-assets/images/AWS-Solutions-Architect-Associate-Certification-Setting-up-your-own-AWS-Account-A-walkthrough/aws-signup-free-tier-form.jpg)

## Step 4: Credit Card and Identity Verification

Next, you'll be asked to provide a credit card for identity verification. AWS might place a temporary hold (typically up to $1 USD) on your card during this process. Rest assured, this is standard procedure, and your actual credit card information will remain secure.

![The image shows an AWS signup page with a section on secure verification, explaining a temporary hold of up to $1 USD for identity verification. There's also a small video call or streaming window in the bottom right corner.](https://kodekloud.com/kk-media/image/upload/v1752864619/notes-assets/images/AWS-Solutions-Architect-Associate-Certification-Setting-up-your-own-AWS-Account-A-walkthrough/aws-signup-secure-verification.jpg)

After entering your credit card details and proceeding, AWS will prompt you for an additional round of identity verification via phone call or text message. Once you receive the verification code, simply enter it.

![The image shows an AWS signup page prompting the user to confirm their identity by entering a verification code. There are also geometric illustrations and a small video overlay of a person speaking.](https://kodekloud.com/kk-media/image/upload/v1752864620/notes-assets/images/AWS-Solutions-Architect-Associate-Certification-Setting-up-your-own-AWS-Account-A-walkthrough/aws-signup-verification-code-image.jpg)

When prompted, you will also be asked to choose between support plans. For most users, the Basic (free) support plan is sufficient as it offers access to comprehensive online support resources.

0061

This code block represents the verification code you will enter. (In practice, you will receive a unique code specific to your registration.)

## Step 5: Completing Registration and Accessing the AWS Management Console

Once the identity verification process is complete, AWS will process your account setup. Global service activation may take up to 24 hours; you will receive an email notification once your account is fully activated.

![The image shows an AWS registration confirmation page with a congratulatory message for signing up. There is also a small circular video overlay of a person speaking.](https://kodekloud.com/kk-media/image/upload/v1752864621/notes-assets/images/AWS-Solutions-Architect-Associate-Certification-Setting-up-your-own-AWS-Account-A-walkthrough/aws-registration-confirmation-page.jpg)

After activation, log in using your root account credentials (e.g., Michael+AWS1@KodeKloud.com with your chosen password). You should see your account name displayed in the upper right-hand corner, such as "Michael Forrester's KodeKloud demo."

## Final Recommendations

After setting up your AWS account, consider these best practices:

1.  Create an IAM user for everyday activities instead of using your root account.
2.  Set up AWS Budgets by searching for "AWS Budgets" in the AWS Management Console. This tool will notify you if your spending exceeds a specified limit (e.g., $5 or $10).

![The image shows the AWS Management Console with a search for "AWS Budgets," displaying various AWS services and features. A small video overlay in the bottom right corner shows a person speaking.](https://kodekloud.com/kk-media/image/upload/v1752864622/notes-assets/images/AWS-Solutions-Architect-Associate-Certification-Setting-up-your-own-AWS-Account-A-walkthrough/aws-management-console-budgets-video.jpg)

Thank you for following along! You now have your own AWS account and are ready to explore the wide array of services AWS has to offer. Always manage your resources carefully to avoid unexpected charges.
